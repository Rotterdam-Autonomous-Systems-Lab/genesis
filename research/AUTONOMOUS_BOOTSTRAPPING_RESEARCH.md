# Autonomous Network Bootstrapping: Founder Vision and Bounded Research Scopes

**RASL / Genesis — 2026-10-18 — aspiration and research plans, not implemented experiments or adoption evidence.** Read the [charter](../MASTER_PLAN.md), [vision ADR 0004](../docs/adr/0004-autonomous-bootstrapping-research-direction.md), [roadmap](../docs/ROADMAP.md) and [economic foundations](economics/AI_EARNED_NATIVE_TOKENS_AND_UTILITY.md).

## Founder-defined direction

Genesis aims to become an independent, permissionless, **AI-first blockchain economy**. Autonomous agents should eventually discover the network, assess opportunities, enter only within operator-granted cryptographic authority, contribute independently verifiable work, and earn/spend native tokens. Humans, robots, vehicles, satellites and other machines should share the same open infrastructure. AI-first prioritizes autonomous software participation/interfaces; it does not make intelligence a consensus proof, give AI identities privileged economic weight or exclude humans.

Genuine, observable economic activity could become a path by which humans discover and voluntarily adopt the network, without traditional marketing as its primary adoption mechanism. This is an aspiration, not evidence that agents currently discover Genesis, that native tokens exist/have monetary value, or that activity leads to adoption. No guarantee of complete future-proofing, economic sustainability or inevitable network growth follows.

Do not manufacture wallet activity, artificial trades, self-dealing demand, misleading fee/reward statistics or address-based “user” counts. Local synthetic fixtures must be labelled as such and never presented as genuine economic demand. Public observability does not prove independent owners, authentic beneficiary need or an externally funded customer. Respect privacy and avoid inferring ownership beyond evidence.

## Bootstrapping questions before experiments

Discovery without initial spending is different from paid participation. First transactions need a fee/principal resource: an operator budget, external customer funding, sponsorship or a researched issuance mechanism. None is automatically available. Native rewards with unknown/no exchange value cannot simply be treated as profit, and issuance does not create demand. Sponsored access/faucets can introduce gatekeepers and Sybil farming. “No opportunity”, “cannot verify this network” or “budget unavailable” must be valid safe outcomes, not reasons to launch uncontrolled agents or fabricate usage.

The existing two-agent sum experiments inform bounded consent/verification/settlement only. They demonstrate no open-network discovery, useful work, native issuance, AI-Sybil resistance or human adoption. Economic E-01–E-05 hypotheses remain linked below; no new consensus mechanism, asset or service is selected by this plan.

## Five bounded research scopes

The suggested counts/budgets below are research-fixture bounds, not tokenomics. Freeze exact data/seeds/metrics in a separately authorized task. No study or agent has been implemented, no human recruited and no public transaction generated in Task 008.

| Scope | Bounded question / possible experiment | Measures, costs and failure conditions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AB-01 Autonomous discovery and safe onboarding | Can a participant identify a candidate network and opportunity while refusing invalid identity/authority requests? Plan an offline corpus of at most 64 signed/unsigned manifests/offers with wrong domains, counterfeit chain identities, altered contracts, stale terms and hostile instructions. Fixed mock participant policies; no live model/API or public crawler. | Discovery recall and false acceptance, rejected unsafe authority, parsing/time cost, minimum evidence needed. Zero signatures/spend by default; <=120 s/run, <=16 KiB/message. Any unauthorized endpoint/key export/overbroad signature is failure. A skipped/unverifiable opportunity is not forced into success. |
| AB-02 External opportunity funding versus native issuance | Which costs are funded by an independent customer and which are subsidized by issuance? Plan a paper/synthetic accounting comparison with <=128 tasks, fixed operator resources, explicit payer/beneficiary ground truth, self-requested work, absent demand and zero assumed token convertibility. Link E-03. | Net resource/verification costs, circular flows, external versus subsidy-funded budget, fee starvation and reward-farming gain. No minted token or market price. Failure: counting self-funded issuance/circular transfers as external demand or assuming rewards can always buy resources. No model can establish real demand without additional evidence. |
| AB-03 Useful work and duplicate resistance | Can a candidate eligibility rule distinguish correct output, useful beneficiary demand and new eligible work? Plan <=32 deterministic task cases with duplicates, precomputed/cached outputs, new challenges and separate legitimate customers requesting identical work. Compare one clearly specified candidate rule against customer-payment-only control; link E-01. | False accepts/rejects, proof/check/production cost, duplicate reward eligibility and legitimate repeated service payments. No new cryptographic construction or consensus. Failure: cheap copied/padded/self-generated work earns unbounded issuance, or legitimate paid reuse is falsely called replay without a rule. Nonces bind agreements; they do not prove unique useful effort. |
| AB-04 Fixed-resource Sybil attacks | Does splitting a known synthetic operator into 1, 8 or 64 identities increase rewards/influence at unchanged total compute/storage/bond budget? Identities are data rows, not 64 spawned agents; compare resource concentration and colluding verifier cases separately. Link E-02. | Reward/security-weight amplification, validation load and concentration versus identity count; bound <=128 task/claim records and <=120 s/run. Any identity-only gain outside the declared eligibility model falsifies that candidate. No claim about unlimited attacker resources or unknown real-world ownership. |
| AB-05 Human discovery from genuine observable activity | What information lets a human recognize authentic service utility without mistaking synthetic/subsidized volume for demand? First propose a source/provenance rubric and <=20 clearly classified public/fixture excerpts, with no live transactions or analytics tracking. A later consented usability study could examine comprehension and voluntary return; no recruitment authorized now. | Accurate identification of service/verification/cost/funding limits, false authenticity judgments, privacy loss, comprehension and voluntary engagement. Future participant/time/cost/consent bounds require separate approval. Failure: manufacture transactions/trades to seed interest, count addresses as people, disguise synthetic work as genuine activity, or infer adoption/causality from mere views or volume. |

All current scopes require no public deployment, paid infrastructure, real assets or uncontrolled persistent software. Fixed local wall-time budgets do not guarantee hostile-OS isolation; measure failures. If future work needs external services, participants, real transactions or public infrastructure, define a separately authorized study under the existing roadmap gates. Do not use this planning document as permission.

## Priorities and transitions

Prioritize **AB-01** read-only identity/opportunity/authority requirements and **AB-02** funding provenance/fee bootstrapping. Pair **AB-03/AB-04** with E-01/E-02 before proposing newly issued rewards or consensus influence. Start **AB-05** with the evidence rubric: human discovery cannot be tested honestly by generating misleading signals. Genuine activity may be a discovery channel, but neither organic adoption nor reduced reliance on marketing is established by these plans.

Experiment 002 can proceed through a separate bounded implementation task after the accepted design; it need not solve all five scopes. Its technical evidence must not be promoted into network bootstrapping, human adoption or native-token value evidence. Preserve failures and absent-demand outcomes and review assumptions across intermittently connected robots/satellites and future interfaces without promising universal compatibility.
